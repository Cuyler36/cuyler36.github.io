*Animal Crossing Deluxe* expands the original GameCube release with restored and adapted content from other versions of Animal Crossing, new gameplay systems, quality-of-life improvements, additional customization, and numerous smaller fixes and enhancements.

Some features are still in development and may change in future releases.

## Player customization

- Skin tone can be selected during character creation.
- The skin tone and tanning system has been overhauled. Each skin tone has eight tanning levels.
- Boy player characters can wear the princess hat, and girl player characters can wear the viking hat.

## Storage, saving, and menus

- Storage has been overhauled to work more like *Wild World* and later games, with 160 storage slots per player.
- Saving and loading are faster than in the original game.
- "Before I go..." has been renamed to "Settings."
- Settings menu options have been rearranged for easier navigation.
- Press **Z + Start** to quicksave.
- Nook's catalog can use either the original sorting order or alphabetical sorting.
- Letters and patterns use their own dedicated files on the Memory Card.

## Display, camera, and performance

- Proper widescreen support has been added.
- Acre-to-acre camera transitions can be disabled.
- The outdoor camera angle can be adjusted with the C-stick.
- A more detailed town map can be viewed by pressing **R**.
- Higher frame rates are supported, up to 200 FPS.
  - **Work in progress:** some systems have not yet been fully converted to use delta time.
- Catching a bug, finding a fossil, and similar actions no longer cause their fanfares to interrupt the hourly music.

## Controls and shortcuts

- Keyboard input is supported.
  - **Dolphin:** any USB keyboard.
  - **Original hardware:** PSO keyboard.
- Menu tabs can be switched backward with **Z + R**.
- Tools can be swapped or put away using the D-pad.
- Holes can be filled without equipping a shovel.
- Trees can be shaken while a net or fishing rod is equipped.
- On the time-change screen, press **L + R + Z** to synchronize the in-game clock.

### Pattern shortcuts

- Press **Z + B** to remove a pattern from the ground.
- Press **Z + D-pad Up** to automatically place the most recently used design again.
- Hold **Z** while using a design tool to access transparency while editing a pattern.

## Town and overworld changes

- A Game Boy Advance is no longer required to visit the island. Kapp'n is always available at the dock.
- Furniture can be placed outdoors.
- Bugs and fish can be dropped on the ground.
- Player-dug holes no longer disappear because of distance.
- More acre types are valid locations for Tortimer to build the third bridge.
- The chance of a villager moving away after visiting another town has been reduced from 100% to 10%.
- Villager quests can progress even when the relevant villager is inside an igloo.
- The wishing well provides additional hints when the town has not yet achieved perfect-town status.
- The requirements for washing Gracie's car have been reduced by 20%.
- The gyroid outside a player's house can be given a nickname.
- Feng shui colors for furniture have been updated to match later Animal Crossing games.

## Custom patterns

- Patterns can be imported and exported through the Memory Card, allowing designs to be shared.
- Patterns can be placed on the ground.
- Patterns can use transparency.
- Additional color palettes can be unlocked by befriending Sable.
- A town flag has been added next to the train station and can display a custom pattern.
- A custom pattern can be used as the background of the item screen.
- Town tunes can be entered using a keyboard.

## Plants and gardening

- Chestnuts have been added as a new fruit type. Chestnuts were originally a scrapped *Doubutsu no Mori* item.
- Flower mechanics have been overhauled and work similarly to, though not exactly like, *Wild World* and later games.
  - More detailed information about the flower system can be found in the dedicated plant documentation.
- Flowers can be dug up and replanted using a shovel.
- Roses have been added.
- Jacob's ladders, originally introduced in *e+*, have been added.
- Hybrid flowers can be bred using a watering can sold by Tom Nook.

## Housing and furniture

- The top floor of the player's house can be expanded to 8×8.
- Basement wallpaper and flooring can be changed.
- Music-player volume can be raised or lowered inside houses.
- More furniture can be used as a surface for tabletop items.
- The control for turning house lights on and off has been moved to the D-pad.
- Gyroids can be placed on tables.
- Tabletop items can be rotated after being placed.
- Rotated diaries no longer have their positions reset.
- The player can change the direction they are facing while seated on four-sided chairs.
  - This also applies to stumps and rocks.

## Tom Nook

- Tom Nook sells one K.K. Slider song each Sunday.
- The player can decline to work for Tom Nook.
- The player can decline to have a statue built after fully paying off their loan.
- Nookington's no longer requires a second Memory Card to unlock.
- Nook's catalog can be switched between its original sorting order and alphabetical sorting.

## Crazy Redd

- Crazy Redd sells four items instead of three.
- Crazy Redd is guaranteed to offer at least one painting during each visit.

## K.K. Slider

- All K.K. Slider songs through *Animal Crossing: New Leaf* have been added.
- Song requests are more forgiving of minor differences when typing a song title.

## Clock and HUD

- The clock interface can be changed to resemble the clocks used in *Doubutsu no Mori*, *Doubutsu no Mori+*, and *Doubutsu no Mori e+*.
- The current acre is displayed near the clock.
  - Currently, this is supported only by the default clock style.

## Fossils

- Blathers can assess fossils after the player has donated 15 fossils.
- Once fossil assessment has been unlocked, fossils can still be mailed to the Farway Museum to be sold.
  - Multi-piece fossils have a higher sale value when sold this way.
- Five additional fossils from *Animal Crossing: Wild World* have been added.

## Golden tools

- The golden watering can is now the reward for achieving a perfect town.
- The golden axe is obtained after purchasing 100 saplings from Tom Nook.

## NES games

- NES games can use different aspect ratios.
- The "Forbidden Four" NES games can be obtained through normal gameplay.

## Letters

- Letters can be thrown away while indoors.
- Multiple letters can be selected at once for:
  - Mailing.
  - Marking as read.
  - Receiving enclosed gifts.
  - Throwing away.

## Balloon presents

- Balloon presents play a sound effect as they float past.
- Balloons are more likely to collide with trees now that a collision bug from the original game has been fixed.
- Balloons can be knocked down with a net while standing at the edge of a cliff.
- Mario items can be obtained from balloon presents.

# Content and mechanics from other Animal Crossing releases

In addition to Deluxe's original changes, a number of features and pieces of content have been restored or adapted from other versions of Animal Crossing.

## Doubutsu no Mori / Doubutsu no Mori+

- Scrapped furniture and clothing from *Doubutsu no Mori* and *Doubutsu no Mori+* have been restored.
- Japan-exclusive furniture has been added.
- A fireworks festival takes place every Saturday in August.
  - The existing July 4th fireworks festival is still present.
- Newly created towns have a 50% chance of generating a shrine instead of the wishing well, as in *Doubutsu no Mori* and *Doubutsu no Mori+*.
  - Towns with a shrine use the Japanese version of the New Year's celebration.

## Doubutsu no Mori e+

- Outdoor decorations from *e+* have been added and can be obtained from a new visiting NPC named Renna.
- The villager move-out system has been overhauled to work like the system in *e+*.
  - See Nookipedia for additional details on the *e+* move-out system.
- Resetti's Surveillance Center has been added.
- Tom Nook can be woken up to access his store at night.
- Players can become best friends with villagers, who will greet the player as they pass by.
- Best-friend dialogue and interactions from *e+* have been added, including greetings, live-song gifts, and other interactions.
- Villagers, furniture, bugs, fish, and K.K. Slider songs from *e+* have been added.
- Sale Day has been overhauled to work like its *e+* version.
- During the Fishing Tourney, Chip gives the player a fishing rod if they do not already have one.
- Letters and patterns use dedicated Memory Card files.

## iQue

- iQue-exclusive furniture and shirts have been added.

## Animal Crossing: Wild World

- Wallpaper and flooring from *Wild World* have been added.
- Clovers have been added.
- Rafflesias have been added.
- Turnip prices can now change between the A.M. and P.M. periods.
- Flower mechanics have been overhauled to work more like *Wild World* and later games.
- Mushroom furniture from *Wild World* has been added and can be found while mushrooming.
- Additional standalone furniture from later games is being added.
  - **Work in progress.**
- Villagers introduced in *Wild World* and later games are being added.
  - **Work in progress.**
- Monkey villagers from *Wild World* are present.
- Holes can be filled without using a shovel.

## Animal Crossing: City Folk

- Tools can be swapped or put away using the D-pad.

## Animal Crossing: New Leaf

- Ordinances have been added.
- Patterned tree stumps have been added.
- Fruit, mushrooms, and candy can be stacked in groups of up to 10 when picked up.
- Players can choose from four different town layouts during town creation.
- Trees can be shaken while a net or fishing rod is equipped.
